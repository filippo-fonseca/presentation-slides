## What changed from v2 (read this)
- **No formal Ask slide.** It's a scout intro, not a raise. No round size, use of funds, or valuation. The close is vision + "here's where we are, let's talk."
- **Honest stage.** Pre-seed, very new, **no hardware prototype yet**. The *intelligence* is the proven asset (Spatial-MemER + iSpy, YHack + Google AI Hackathon @ Yale 2026 wins). The hardware is the next build, co-developed with farmers. We frame hardware risk as a *plan*, not a hole.
- **Crop-agnostic is a headline, not a footnote.** No beachhead-crop bet. The agentic layer is general-purpose and modular: it works across crops and farmers. Hammer this (it's the opposite of single-crop competitors like Aigen).
- **Real team.** Filippo Fonseca (Mechanical Eng. ABET + EECS), Emir Ahmed (EECS), David Antwi (EECS) — Yale '28. (Mariia dropped; Ocura was a prior non-company vehicle, don't dwell.)

## Voice & design (from the research + Also's portfolio)
- Pre-seed reality: ~10-14 slides, ~20s/slide, ~58% reach the end → front-load, no padding. **Team + "why now" insight carry pre-seed**; product slide earns the most attention; **evidence substitutes for traction** (the brain, the wins, the farmer conversations).
- Also voice: short declarative claim + concrete mechanism; nouns & numbers, not adjectives; no exclamation marks; never "agtech." Anti-entropy spine: chemical farming *amplifies* decay; Tendril *resists* it.
- Design: one idea per slide; beautiful, uncluttered; hammer points without repeating them; reuse the landing's components (Fraunces serif, Space Mono kickers, the swarm visual, the `.farm` file mock, glass cards, persimmon/chartreuse on soil-black).

"Smart" precision package ~$10-15K hardware + **annual software subscriptions** (Operations Center). **Full autonomy:** ~$40K autonomy-ready package + ~$10K/yr per-license → **~$50K first year on top of the tractor.** Compact/utility tractors run $15K-$24K (small scale only).
- **Carbon Robotics LaserWeeder G2:** a pull-behind implement, **~$600K to $1.6M** depending on width (20ft vs 40ft). Lasers + computer vision; weeding only. (Also sells Carbon ATK, an autonomous retrofit kit for Deere 6R/8R.)

## Slide-by-slide (~14)

### 1 — Cover
Mark; pill "For Also Capital". Title: *The physically intelligent operating system for the* **open field.** Sub: "A crop-agnostic, agentic farm OS, delivered through small, chemical-free robots." Attribution: "Filippo Fonseca, Emir Ahmed, David Antwi · Yale · 2026". *Note: keep the scout's name out; this is sendable.*

### 2 — The thesis (what Tendril is)
- Headline: "The physically intelligent operating system for the **open field.**"
- The three differentiators: **Cohesive & crop-agnostic** (one general-purpose system for daily work *and* the season-long plan, not single-task gadgets, not one-crop bets) · **Agentic** (perceives, remembers, decides, acts) · **Built with the grower** (for the small family farms that are most of US agriculture).
- Shape: "A swarm of small, modular, chemical-free robots. The chemical treadmill is what we replace."

### 3 — The problem (entropy, made literal)
- Headline: "Chemical farming **amplifies** the decay it's meant to fight."
- Stats: **548** resistant weeds, climbing · **0** new modes of action since the 1980s · **50-70%** of specialty cost is labor.
- Line: "Spray more, on a calendar, across acres no one sees plant by plant. Resistance compounds, soil thins, inputs escalate. That is decay, amplified."

### 4 — Why now (the insight)
The non-generic insight, four doors: **edge vision got cheap** (per-plant classification on a robot, no datacenter) · **the intelligence layer now exists** (we built it) · **chemistry is being pulled** faster than it's replaced · **robotics stayed funded** (supply chain matured). Punch: "Unbuildable five years ago. Table stakes in five. The window is now."

### 5 — The team that already built the brain  *(moved up: most-weighted at pre-seed)*
- Headline: "We didn't start with robots. **We started with the brain.**"
- Three founders (photos + one credential each): Filippo Fonseca (Mechanical Eng. ABET + EECS), Emir Ahmed (EECS), David Antwi (EECS), Yale '28.
- Proof it's real: **Spatial-MemER** — persistent spatial memory, built with Stanford researchers · **iSpy** — the visual backbone · winners of **YHack** and the **Google AI Hackathon @ Yale 2026**.
- Honest gap: "We're not agronomists yet. We're closing that the only honest way: talking to and partnering with the farmers who'd use this, right now."
- FMF line: "A hardware company would have to build the intelligence from scratch. We start from it, and give it hands."

### 6 — The system (the agentic loop + its inputs)
- Headline: "Small machines. One **agentic loop.** Every acre, any crop."
- Loop: **Perceive → Remember → Decide → Act** (the landing's swarm visual). Diverse terrestrial inputs feeding one core: the robot swarm, stationary cameras, soil & weather sensors, the grower, building a live 3D field map.
- Hammer crop-agnostic: "The core doesn't care what's growing. The same loop runs corn, cotton, tomatoes, vines: swap the limb, not the brain."

### 6b — What the hands actually do  *(NEW — make the physical capability concrete)*
- Headline: "The brain is the company. **But the hands do real work.**"
- The modular tools (one chassis, swap the limb): **Weed** — mechanical, chemical-free removal, plant by plant (the v1 job) · **Scout** — leaf-level vision classifying crop / weed / pest / disease / damage, building the live 3D map · **Treat** — place an intervention precisely on the plant that needs it (spot, never blanket); e.g. a UV-C disease module · **Tend** — snap in the tool the season calls for; same chassis, many jobs, all season.
- Why it matters here: "This is what makes the funnel real. A dashboard tells you a weed is there. **Our hand pulls it.**"
- Honest framing: v1 leads with weeding + perception; the other modules are the near-term roadmap on the same proven loop. Hardware in development.

### 7 — The .farm file + the funnel
- Headline: "Every day on the farm compiles to **one file.**"
- Animated `.farm` mock (from the landing). Left: aggregative, interpretable, exportable, dynamic. The funnel: complex inputs → one `.farm` file → the agentic layer decides *with* the grower → **execution isn't abstract** (real robots actuate). Punch: "Execution, not advice."
- Honest status line (small): "The intelligence is built and proven. The robots are in development, co-developed with growers."

### 8 — Small and modular (the approach that worked for power)
- Headline: "Small and modular. **Like microreactors, not megaprojects.**"
- Mass-manufacturable · modular (snap in a weeder, a scout, a disease arm) · affordable (priced on the acre, zero capex). Contrast with the bulky incumbents: "A Deere row-crop tractor is **$500-600K**, plus ~$50K and annual subscriptions just to switch on autonomy, and still no agentic farm OS. A Carbon LaserWeeder is **$600K-$1.6M** and only weeds. We're one of many small units, priced on the acre." *(Radiant/Kaleidos resonance for Also: small, mass-manufacturable, modular beats big and centralized.)*

### 9 — How we're different
- Headline: "Big and bulky, or blind. **We're the only true farm OS.**"
- Cost contrast (concrete, hammers affordability): **Deere** $500-600K + ~$50K/yr to switch on autonomy · **Carbon LaserWeeder** $600K-$1.6M, weeds only · **Aigen** small but **mechanical weeding only**, a single-task rover · **Tendril** $0 capex, priced per acre.
- Table (Capability | Tendril | the field): resolution per plant vs per field · chemistry mechanical/chemical-free vs herbicide · scope **whole farm, any crop** vs one job/one crop · memory persistent vs none · decisioning **agentic** vs operator/dashboard · capital **$0, per-acre** vs $500K-$1.6M+.
- The shape no one else has: "A swarm that is the **hands, eyes, and ears** of the farmer, run by one agentic, crop-agnostic OS. The incumbents are either big, bulky, single-purpose machines (Deere, Carbon, Aigen) or dashboards with no hands (INCYT, the IoT crowd). We're neither." Comps bar (Carbon 177, Ecorobotix 150, Verdant 46, Aigen 19) proves the market is funded; none own our shape.

### 10 — The moat
- Headline: "Hardware gets copied. **A field that remembers does not.**" *(reserved for the deck.)*
- Persistent field memory · compounding 3D per-plant multi-season dataset · switching cost by outcome · the dataset is itself a revenue line. Pull-quote: "The robots are the hands. The memory is the company."

### 11 — Who we build with (market, concrete not inflated)
- Headline: "Built **with and for** the small family farms that are most of US agriculture."
- ~**96%** family-owned, ~**1.9M** farms, 86% small. Crop-agnostic = every one of them is addressable, not one crop's budget. Reachable, squeezed, underserved (no procurement, no dealer gate). *Keep qualitative-but-specific; no fake bottom-up TAM in a teaser.*

### 12 — Business model (brief)
- Headline: "Robotics-as-a-Service. **We own the fleet, the uptime, and the data.**"
- No grower capex · priced per acre tended · recurring by construction · data flywheel. Attacks the farm's single biggest line (the iron: ~$450K tractor, ~$1M combine).

### 13 — The stakes
- Headline: "Ten billion people by 2060. **The same land, or less.**"
- We can't deforest our way there; efficiency per acre is the only lever. "Food is the hardest, most necessary, most durable problem there is, and we're building the system that resists decay in the field."

### 14 — Close (no ask, scout-appropriate)
- Headline: "Step off the **treadmill.**"
- Where we are (honest): "Very early. The intelligence is proven; the swarm is in development; we're partnering with farmers now." Then: "If you back founders who choose hard problems, we'd love to talk." Contact: filippo.fonseca@yale.edu. *No round size, no use of funds.*
