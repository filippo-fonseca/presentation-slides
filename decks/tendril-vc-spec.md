## Why this framing (the strategy)

Also Capital funds **"founders building systems that resist decay rather than amplify it"** and things that **"push back against entropy rather than ride the latest wave."** Their get-in-touch line: *"If you are working on something that is hard, necessary, and durable… we would like to hear from you."* They run concentrated funds (~15 core companies at Inception), help build the "First Five," and back small-modular hard tech (e.g. **Radiant / Kaleidos**: mass-manufactured nuclear microreactors replacing diesel generators).

Tendril's enemy is, literally, entropy in a field: herbicide resistance compounds, soil biology collapses, inputs escalate. Chemical agriculture **amplifies** decay; Tendril **resists** it. That is the spine of this deck.

**Three positioning pillars (lead with these everywhere):**
1. **Cohesive / general-purpose.** One system for the daily work *and* the season-long plan, not an assortment of single-task gadgets.
2. **Agentic & physically intelligent.** It perceives, remembers, decides, and acts. It doesn't just show you data, and because there are real robots in the field, the execution isn't abstract.
3. **Built with and for small family farms** — ~96% of US agriculture — that can't afford a $400K+ John Deere smart tractor (which doesn't even ship with an agentic OS).

**Voice rules (from Also's portfolio):** short declarative claim + a concrete mechanism; nouns and numbers, not adjectives; no exclamation marks, no emoji; never the word "agtech" (say "agricultural infrastructure," "a farm operating system," "hardware for the field"). Litmus: would the line sit between K2's "Build Bigger" and Varda's "Space born, Earth bound"?
